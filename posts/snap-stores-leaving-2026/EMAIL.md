## 3. Body, part one

```
On August 28, we asked what would happen to SNAP stores when the new stocking rule took effect. Maybe nothing. Maybe SNAP-authorized retail would be cut by a third. We said we would track USDA's retailer list after the rule landed and report what changed.

But it turns out that while we were bracing ourselves for the fallout from the stocking rule, stores were already leaving SNAP faster than usual in 2026. In 2026, 21,923 stores left SNAP by November 5. That is 8.8% of the stores authorized at the start of the year. In 2020 through 2024, between 8,872 and 11,215 stores left over the same months.
```

## 5. Body, part two

```
Independent convenience stores led the rise. In 2026, 9,290 of them left SNAP, 18.2% of those authorized at the start of the year. In 2019 through 2025, that share ranged from 7.7% to 11.5%.

No place lost more convenience stores and small groceries from SNAP than New York City, where many of them are the corner stores known as bodegas. The city lost 1,368. Harris County, Texas, was next, with 518.

Many things changed for SNAP in 2026 at about the same time, so the data cannot show which ones led to these exits. It does show that both the stores in SNAP and the number of people using it changed a lot this year.

The full report has a map of every SNAP store, so you can see what happened in your community. It also looks at what Arizona's drop in enrollment may signal for Medicaid.
```
