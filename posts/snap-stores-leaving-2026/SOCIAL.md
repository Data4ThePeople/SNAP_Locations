```
Between January 1 and November 5, 2026, 21,923 stores left SNAP. That is 8.8% of the stores authorized at the start of the year, and 88% more than the 2019 to 2025 average.

On August 28, we asked what would happen to SNAP stores when USDA's new stocking rule took effect. It turns out that was the wrong question. Nearly all of these exits happened before the rule took effect on November 4.

What stood out in USDA's retailer data:

- Independent convenience stores led the rise. 9,290 left SNAP, 18.2% of those authorized. From 2019 through 2025, that share ranged from 7.7% to 11.5%.
- No place lost more convenience stores and small groceries than New York City, where many are bodegas. The city lost 1,368, or 29.6% of those in the city.
- Fewer stores are coming in. In 2026, 2.50 stores left SNAP for every new one. From 2020 through 2024, it was 0.64 to 0.80.
- 22 counties now have no SNAP store, and 37 have one.

At the same time, SNAP enrollment fell 13.2% from July 2025 to July 2026. Several things changed for SNAP at once, and the data shows where stores left, not why. But if you care about SNAP, this is worth paying attention to.

The full report and a map of every SNAP store are linked in the first comment.
```
