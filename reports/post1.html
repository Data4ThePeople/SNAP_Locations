The dollar store did not out-grow the grocery store. It out-lived it.

SNAP-authorized retailers, 2006–2025 · USDA Food and Nutrition Service historical authorization records · 37,361 dollar stores accepted SNAP at the end of 2025

4,024ZIP codes now have a dollar store and no grocery of any size

Everyone knows dollar stores grew. The interesting part is how. Take every store that first entered the SNAP program between 2008 and 2012, and ask how many are still in it at the end of 2025.

A dollar store from that cohort is 17.4× more likely to still be operating than a small grocer from the same years. Small grocery is the extreme case: 717 survivors from 15,800 entrants. Ninety-five percent are gone.

This is not the shape people usually assume. The story is generally told as dollar stores opening aggressively, and they do open steadily — but on that measure they are not even unusual. Supermarket openings are steadier year to year than dollar store openings. What is unusual is that dollar stores essentially never close.

Fifteen years of one direction

The result compounds. Roughly 1,760 new dollar store authorizations a year against about 253 closures produces a line that only goes up.

Set against the grocery formats, the crossing is hard to miss. Dollar stores passed every individual grocery format years ago and are now the second most common kind of SNAP retailer in the country, behind convenience stores only.

2024 is the first break in the pattern

Closures jumped from a few hundred a year to 1,269 in 2024 — and this is one place the data lines up with events that made the news.

Dollar Tree spent 2024 closing Family Dollar locations, and 99 Cents Only liquidated entirely that spring. Both show up here as authorization endings, which is a useful check: when something verifiable happens in the retail world, this dataset sees it.

Where this actually matters

Growth in the aggregate is not the same as growth where it counts. The sharper question is how often a dollar store is the only thing there.

In 2008 that described 675 ZIP codes. By 2024 it described 4,024 — 26% of every ZIP code that has a dollar store at all. Nationally there are now 37,361 dollar stores against 63,497 grocery stores of every size combined.

What this data can and cannot say

These are SNAP authorizations, not stores. A retailer that closes and one that merely stops accepting SNAP look identical here, so "left the program" is the honest reading of every exit.

Nothing here measures floor space, sales, or what is actually on the shelves. A dollar store and a supermarket count as one record each. USDA's own classification is by stocking breadth, and the 2016 stocking-standards rule changed that bar mid-series — which matters more for grocery formats than for dollar stores.

Store-level SNAP redemption dollars are not public: Food Marketing Institute v. Argus Leader (2019) closed that under FOIA Exemption 4. We can see where retailers are, never how much each transacts.
